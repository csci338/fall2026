# DRAFT — Late Programming Readiness Pathway Options

> **DEPARTMENT/CHAIR REVIEW REQUIRED BEFORE ADOPTION OR COMMUNICATION**
>
> This document is a discussion draft, not an approved policy. It makes no claim about institutional requirements, enrollment consequences, grading authority, accommodations, academic standing, withdrawal, or student eligibility. Confirm all language, dates, supports, records practices, and consequences with the department chair and the appropriate institutional offices before use.

## Purpose for review

Identify a fair, instructionally sound pathway for a student whose required **Programming Readiness Bundle** remains incomplete after the September 24 verification/reassessment. The bundle uses binary **Satisfactory / Not Yet** specifications, no points or averaging, and banked Satisfactory specifications. Any pathway should reassess only Not Yet specifications with fresh equivalent evidence.

## Nonnegotiable design principles proposed for review

- Preserve banked Satisfactory results.
- Give written, specification-level evidence and a focused module prescription for each Not Yet result.
- Use secure alternate tasks/forms and observed individual verification.
- Apply approved accommodations and provide comparable access to practice and reassessment.
- Separate readiness evidence from any academic-integrity process.
- Do not use AI detectors. When authorship is uncertain, use observed tracing, explanation, testing, or revision of comparable work.
- Publish any approved deadlines and consequences before they apply.
- Keep private records of attempts, evidence, prescriptions, and outcomes.

## Option A — One structured late reassessment

After September 24, offer one scheduled reassessment window for only the remaining Not Yet specifications.

Possible conditions for reviewers to decide:

- completion of each assigned practice module before scheduling;
- an instructor or tutoring check-in;
- a secure alternate paper form for R1–R5;
- a supplied-harness follow-up for R5/R6; and
- a final date that leaves time for notice and support.

Questions: Is one window sufficiently accessible? What happens after an excused absence? Who can administer alternate-time accommodations?

## Option B — Two-stage completion conference

Stage 1 is a short observed paper reassessment of remaining R1–R4/R5 specifications. Stage 2 is an observed typed harness task for remaining R5/R6 specifications. Schedule stages separately to reduce cognitive load.

Questions: Is staffing available? How close together must stages occur? What evidence is retained? Does separating stages create unequal access?

## Option C — Individual readiness plan

Create a time-limited plan listing each Not Yet specification, assigned action, check-in, alternate verification, and completion date. This offers flexibility while keeping binary criteria unchanged.

Questions: Who approves plans? What range of dates is equitable? How are plans documented consistently? What limits prevent indefinite extensions while still honoring approved accommodations?

## Option D — Course-structure response

If the bundle remains incomplete after approved supports and reassessments, reviewers may consider whether an adjustment to later course participation is educationally appropriate.

No specific consequence is proposed here. Before considering this option, obtain authoritative review of:

- syllabus and catalog consistency;
- grading and enrollment rules;
- financial-aid, attendance, and withdrawal implications;
- disability accommodations and equal-access obligations;
- advising and student-notification procedures; and
- whether “team eligible” or access to later work may be conditioned on bundle completion.

Do not communicate or apply a restriction based on this draft.

## Proposed record language

Until an option is approved, record only the evidence:

> Programming Readiness Bundle not yet complete. Satisfactory specifications remain banked. Current Not Yet specifications: ________. Module/action prescribed: ________. Next available step is pending approved course/department guidance.

This wording intentionally avoids claiming a consequence that has not been reviewed and approved.
